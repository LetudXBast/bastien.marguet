Based on your answers to the survey.
Starting point

You underestimate yourself

  • Non-PhDs describe PhDs positively
  • PhDs expect more negative perceptions

Gap: you think you are perceived worse than you actually are.

Key result

The problem is not competence

  • Competence: clearly recognized
  • Social perception: neutral or unclear

Translation: you are seen as capable, but not always as adaptable.

Insight

A small but real gap

  • PhDs are considered employable
  • But engineers are slightly preferred

≈ -6 points in employability

Not a rejection — a perception issue.

Data

So what is the real issue?

You are not lacking skills.

You are lacking translation.

Your value exists.

But it is not clearly expressed.

Turning point

Example

PhD language:
“I worked on a complex research problem.”

Market language:
“I led a 3-year project under uncertainty with no predefined solution.”

Translation

What did you actually learn?

  • Working under uncertainty
  • Structuring complex problems
  • Managing long-term projects
  • Dealing with failure and doubt
Skills

Now your turn

In 2 sentences:

“I am useful for a company because…”

No jargon. No thesis title.

Only value.

Exercise

Final question

If you had to leave academia tomorrow:

What would you sell — and how?

Takeaway

How employable do you feel?

Average score: 6.8 / 10

“Yes, I am employable”: 72%

Most of you feel relatively confident.

For which job?

  • “Consulting”
  • “R&D”

  • “Industry”

Very few precise roles.

What do you actually sell?

  • “Rigor”
  • “Analysis”
  • “Expertise”
  • “Autonomy”

These are not what companies buy.

How do you think recruiters see you?

  • “Too theoretical”
  • “Not adapted”
  • “Overqualified”

You expect a negative perception.

Reality is different

Non-PhDs describe PhDs positively.

PhDs expect more negative perceptions.

You underestimate your value.

So what is the real problem?

You feel employable.

You have strong skills.

But you cannot express them clearly.

The problem is not your value.

It is your ability to translate it.
